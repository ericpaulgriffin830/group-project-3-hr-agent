# Northbridge Analytics Employee Handbook — Overview

Northbridge Analytics, Inc. ("Northbridge," "the Company") is a data and analytics consulting firm headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, with employees working from Company offices, hybrid arrangements, and fully remote arrangements across the United States. This handbook is the starting point for all Company policies and procedures referenced throughout an employee's time at Northbridge, and it explains how the rest of the policy corpus is organized so that employees, managers, and internal tools can navigate it consistently.

## HANDBOOK-1 Purpose and Scope

This document, together with the other policy documents in the Northbridge policy corpus, governs the employment relationship between Northbridge and its employees. It applies to all full-time and part-time employees. Independent contractors and temporary agency staff are covered only where a policy explicitly states that it applies to contractors — for example, the Information Security Policy applies to contractors because they may access Company systems, while the PTO and Holidays Policy does not because contractors are not eligible for PTO. Where a specific policy document conflicts with a general statement in this overview, the specific policy document controls, since this overview is intentionally high-level and is updated less frequently than the operational policies it introduces.

This handbook does not constitute an employment contract and does not alter the at-will nature of employment recognized in most U.S. states, except where a signed individual agreement or local law provides otherwise. Nothing in this handbook or the accompanying policy documents should be read as a guarantee of continued employment, a specific work assignment, or a specific compensation structure; those matters are addressed in an employee's individual offer letter and any applicable compensation plan documents, which are outside the scope of this policy corpus.

Employees who have questions about how this handbook interacts with a separate agreement they have signed — such as a relocation agreement, an equity agreement, or a client-specific confidentiality agreement — should raise the question with People Operations through an HR ticket (see HR-OPS-2) rather than assume the general policy language resolves the question, since individually negotiated terms can and do take precedence over the general handbook in the specific areas they cover.

## HANDBOOK-2 Company Overview

Northbridge was founded in 2014 and has grown to approximately 340 employees across four U.S. office locations: Chicago (headquarters), Austin, Denver, and Raleigh. Roughly half of Northbridge employees work in a hybrid arrangement, a quarter work fully remotely, and the remainder work primarily from a Company office. Departments include Consulting Delivery, Data Engineering, Platform Engineering, Sales, Marketing, Finance, and People Operations (referred to as "HR" or "People Ops" throughout these documents).

Northbridge's client base consists primarily of mid-market and enterprise organizations engaging the Company for data strategy, analytics platform implementation, and applied AI consulting engagements. Because many engagements involve access to client systems and client data, several Northbridge policies — particularly the Information Security Policy and portions of the Workplace Conduct Policy — apply additional expectations to client-facing roles beyond what a purely internal role might require. Engagement leads are responsible for communicating any client-specific requirements that go beyond the baseline set in this corpus, and those client-specific requirements do not appear in this corpus because they vary by client and by contract.

The Company's fiscal year runs from January 1 through December 31, which is relevant to several policies in this corpus that operate on an annual cycle, including PTO carryover (PTO-4), floating holidays (PTO-6), the home office stipend renewal (EXP-2), and open enrollment for benefits (BEN-8).

## HANDBOOK-3 How to Use This Handbook

Each policy document in the corpus is identified by a short document code (for example, PTO-HOLIDAYS or REMOTE-WORK) and contains numbered sections (for example, PTO-3). When Northbridge systems, tools, or HR staff cite a policy, they will reference the document code and section number so that employees and automated tools can locate the exact source of a statement. Employees should treat numbered sections as the authoritative unit of policy; headings and surrounding prose are for readability only. A single employee question often touches more than one document — for example, a question about taking parental leave draws on both the Leave of Absence Policy (for eligibility and duration) and the PTO and Holidays Policy (for how PTO interacts with unpaid portions of leave), so employees and any assistant tooling built on this corpus should expect and look for cross-references rather than assuming a single document contains the complete answer.

Policies are reviewed at least annually and may be updated more frequently in response to legal changes or operational needs. The version and effective date at the top of each document indicate the current, authoritative version. Superseded versions are removed from the active corpus and archived separately by People Operations; only the current version is loaded into the Company's HR knowledge base and assistant tools. Employees relying on a downloaded or printed copy of any policy should confirm they are looking at the current version by checking the effective date against the version published in the internal HR portal, since a printed copy does not automatically update.

## HANDBOOK-5 Policy Review and Updates

Employees are notified of material policy changes by email and by an update banner in the internal HR portal. Non-material clarifications (such as fixing a broken cross-reference or updating a job title referenced as an example) may be made without a broad notification, but will still update the document's version number and effective date for traceability. Questions about how a policy applies to a specific situation should be directed to People Operations through an HR ticket (see HR-OPS-2) rather than assumed from general reading of the handbook, since individual circumstances (employment type, location, tenure) can change which provisions apply, and a general document cannot anticipate every combination of circumstances an individual employee might have.

When two versions of the same policy appear to conflict — for instance, because an employee saved a policy PDF from an earlier point in their tenure — the version with the later effective date governs current conduct, though the version in effect at the time of a past event generally governs how that past event is evaluated. People Operations can provide the historical version of a policy on request for this purpose.

## HANDBOOK-6 Definitions

The following terms are used consistently across the policy corpus and are defined here rather than repeated in each document:

- **Employment type**: One of full-time exempt, full-time non-exempt, part-time, or contractor, as recorded in the employee's HR record. Exempt and non-exempt refer to overtime eligibility under the U.S. Fair Labor Standards Act; exempt employees are not eligible for overtime pay, non-exempt employees are. An employee's exempt or non-exempt classification is determined by People Operations based on job duties and applicable law, not by employee or manager preference.
- **Home office**: The Company office to which an employee is administratively assigned, regardless of how often they physically work there. The home office determines certain administrative defaults (such as which office's local holiday calendar, if any, applies) but does not by itself determine tax withholding, which is instead based on actual physical work location per TAX-2.
- **Work arrangement**: One of onsite, hybrid, or remote, describing an employee's expected physical work pattern relative to their home office, as defined in RW-1.
- **Manager of record**: The individual listed as an employee's manager in the HR system, responsible for approvals described throughout these policies. The manager of record may differ temporarily from an employee's day-to-day project lead on a consulting engagement; policy approvals in this corpus (such as PTO approval) are the responsibility of the manager of record unless a document states otherwise.
- **Business day**: Monday through Friday, excluding the Company holidays listed in PTO-5, regardless of an individual employee's work schedule. Notice periods and response-time targets stated in business days throughout this corpus (such as the PTO request notice period in PTO-3) are calculated on this basis.
- **HR ticket**: A request submitted through the internal HR ticketing system described in HR-OPS-1, used as the standard channel for the requests and approvals described throughout this corpus.

## HANDBOOK-7 Contact and Feedback

Questions about the content or interpretation of any document in this corpus should be submitted as a Policy Question HR ticket per HR-OPS-2, rather than raised informally with a manager, so that the answer is recorded consistently and so that People Operations can identify when a policy's wording is causing repeated confusion. People Operations reviews recurring Policy Question themes as part of the annual policy review referenced in HANDBOOK-5, and a pattern of similar questions about a given section is treated as a signal that the section may need to be clarified in the next revision, independent of whether the underlying policy itself is changing.

Suggestions for improving clarity, adding a missing topic, or correcting an error in this corpus are welcome from any employee and can be submitted the same way, addressed to People Operations. Suggestions are not guaranteed to result in a policy change, since some suggestions may involve a substantive trade-off (for example, a longer notice period that improves predictability for managers but reduces flexibility for employees) that requires deliberate review rather than a quick edit, but every suggestion is acknowledged and considered as part of the next scheduled review.
