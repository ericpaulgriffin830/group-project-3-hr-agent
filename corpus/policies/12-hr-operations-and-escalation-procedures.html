HR Operations and Escalation Procedures

Document ID: HR-OPS · Version 1.8 · Effective 2026-01-05 · Owner: People Operations · Applies to: All employees, managers, and People Operations staff

HR-OPS-1 HR Service Model

People Operations handles employee requests primarily through an HR ticketing system, supplemented by direct conversations for sensitive matters. Employees and managers can open a ticket for questions about policy interpretation, benefits changes, leave requests, equipment coordination, and conduct concerns. Tickets are the system of record for tracking HR requests referenced throughout this policy corpus (for example, PTO-3, CONDUCT-3, and RW-3 all route through this ticketing system). Using a consistent ticketing system, rather than ad hoc email or chat messages, ensures that requests are not lost, that response-time targets can be measured, and that a clear record exists if a request is later escalated or disputed.

HR-OPS-2 Ticket Categories and SLAs

HR tickets are categorized as: Policy Question (target response: 2 business days), Benefits (target response: 3 business days), Leave Request (target response: 2 business days, per LEAVE-7), Equipment/IT Coordination (target response: 1 business day), Conduct Concern (target initial response: 1 business day, per CONDUCT-3), and Remote Work / Location Change (target response: 5 business days given the multi-approver process in RW-6). These targets describe the time to an initial substantive response, not necessarily a final decision; a Remote Work / Location Change ticket, for example, may receive an initial response within 5 business days acknowledging the request and outlining next steps, even where the full Finance and Legal review described in TAX-3 takes longer to complete. Urgent safety concerns should not wait for standard ticket routing and should be escalated per HR-OPS-3.

HR-OPS-3 Escalation Criteria

A request should be escalated beyond standard ticket handling when it involves: an imminent safety risk (see CONDUCT-7), a legal deadline (such as the 3-business-day I-9 window in ONB-6 or a state-mandated leave notice period), a request the assigned HR representative cannot resolve within the category SLA, or a conflict of interest involving a member of the People Operations team itself, which is escalated to the VP of People Operations directly. A ticket that is escalated retains its original category and history; escalation adds a reviewer rather than restarting the request.

HR-OPS-4 Confidentiality of HR Records

HR records, including leave documentation, conduct investigation files, and benefits elections, are Confidential data under SEC-3 and are accessible only to People Operations staff with a documented business need, the employee's direct manager where relevant to an approval decision, and Legal where required. Medical information related to a leave of absence (LEAVE-2, LEAVE-4) is held to a stricter standard and is not shared with an employee's manager beyond the fact that leave has been approved and its expected duration; a manager approving or being informed of a leave request does not receive the underlying medical certification or diagnosis.

HR-OPS-5 Manager Escalation Responsibilities

Managers who receive a report or request that falls outside their authority to resolve — including any conduct concern, any request involving a possible policy violation, or any request they are unsure how to handle — should open an HR ticket or contact People Operations directly rather than attempting to resolve it informally. Managers should not promise a specific outcome (such as approval of a location change or a benefits exception) before the relevant HR, Finance, or Legal review is complete, since doing so can create an expectation the Company may not be able to meet and can complicate the review process described in RW-6.

HR-OPS-6 Emergency Contacts

For an imminent safety threat, employees should first contact local emergency services (911 in the United States) and then notify People Operations as soon as safely possible. A 24-hour People Operations emergency line is available for urgent, non-life-threatening situations such as a serious workplace injury or an urgent leave situation arising outside business hours; the number is published on the internal HR portal home page rather than in this policy corpus, since it may change independently of policy content.

HR-OPS-7 Ticket Lifecycle and Statuses

Tickets move through the following statuses: open (submitted, awaiting initial response), in review (an HR representative or approver is actively working the request, which may include the multi-approver review described in RW-6 for location changes), resolved (a decision has been communicated to the requester), and closed (resolved and no further action is expected). A resolved ticket may be reopened by the original requester within 30 days if new information changes the situation; after 30 days, a new ticket referencing the prior ticket number should be submitted instead.

HR-OPS-8 Roles Within People Operations

Tickets are routed to a People Operations generalist by default, who handles Policy Question, Benefits, and Equipment/IT Coordination categories directly. Leave Request tickets are routed to the leave specialist, who is also responsible for the medical documentation confidentiality standard described in HR-OPS-4. Conduct Concern tickets are routed to a senior People Operations partner, reflecting the sensitivity described in CONDUCT-3 and CONDUCT-4. Remote Work / Location Change tickets are routed jointly to People Operations, a Finance business partner, and IT Security, consistent with the three-part approval process in RW-6, so that all three reviews can proceed without the employee needing to separately track down each approver.

HR-OPS-9 Metrics and Continuous Improvement

People Operations tracks ticket volume, response times against the SLAs in HR-OPS-2, and reopen rates by category on a monthly basis, and reviews trends quarterly with department leadership. A category that consistently misses its SLA target is reviewed to determine whether the SLA itself needs adjustment, whether additional staffing is needed, or whether a process change (such as a clearer self-service answer for a frequently asked Policy Question) could reduce ticket volume in that category. This review process is operational rather than part of the policy itself, and does not change the SLA targets published in HR-OPS-2 unless and until a policy update is issued reflecting a new target.
