# Expense and Reimbursement Policy

## EXP-1 General Principles

Northbridge reimburses reasonable, business-necessary expenses that are properly documented and submitted within 60 days of being incurred. Expenses should be the most economical option that reasonably meets the business need; this does not mean employees must always choose the cheapest available option, but that materially more expensive options should have a reasonable justification (for example, a direct flight that costs more but saves substantial travel time on a short trip). Employees should not incur significant expenses without prior approval where this policy requires it, and should ask their manager or Finance in advance if it is unclear whether an anticipated expense is covered.

Expenses submitted after the 60-day window may still be reviewed at Finance's discretion but are not guaranteed reimbursement; employees who anticipate a delay in submitting (for example, due to an extended leave) should flag this to Finance in advance rather than submitting late without explanation.

## EXP-2 Home Office Equipment and Furniture

Employees whose work arrangement is hybrid or remote (see RW-1) are eligible for a one-time home office stipend of up to $500 within their first 90 days, and up to $250 per year thereafter, for furniture and setup items such as a desk, chair, or monitor arm. The annual $250 stipend does not carry over if unused in a given year, and requires the employee to still hold a hybrid or remote classification at the time of the request; an employee who has since moved to onsite is no longer eligible for the ongoing annual stipend, though this does not claw back amounts already reimbursed. Ergonomic equipment recommended by an occupational health assessment is reimbursed separately and is not subject to the stipend cap, since ergonomic accommodations are treated as a health and safety matter rather than a discretionary setup expense. Items purchased under this stipend are the employee's personal property; they are not tracked as Company equipment under EQ-1, and are not required to be returned upon separation from the Company, unlike the equipment covered in EQ-3.

An employee who spends more than the applicable stipend cap in a single purchase (for example, a $310 monitor against a $250 annual cap) is reimbursed up to the cap, with the remainder either declined or, at manager discretion, covered as a separate exception with appropriate justification; exceptions are not automatic and should be discussed with the manager before the purchase where possible.

## EXP-3 Technology and Software

Laptops, monitors, keyboards, and other core computing equipment are issued directly by IT under the Equipment and Technology Policy (EQUIPMENT) rather than purchased and reimbursed by the employee. Employees should not purchase a personal laptop and expect reimbursement; requests for additional or replacement equipment should go through EQ-2. A personal laptop purchase submitted as an expense is generally denied under this section, with the employee redirected to the equipment request process instead, since the Company's standard practice is to issue equipment directly (which also ensures the security configuration required under SEC-4 is applied) rather than reimburse ad hoc personal purchases of core equipment.

Business software subscriptions under $50/month may be expensed with manager approval; subscriptions above that amount require IT review under EQ-5 to confirm no existing Company license is available and that the software meets the security review standards referenced there. A subscription that renews monthly should generally be requested once as an approved recurring expense rather than resubmitted every month, to reduce administrative overhead for both the employee and Finance.

## EXP-4 Business Travel

Airfare should be booked in economy class for flights under 6 hours and may be booked in premium economy for longer flights, using the Company's designated travel booking tool where available. Ground transportation, reasonable baggage fees, and standard hotel accommodations are reimbursable. Travel expenses over $750 in total for a single trip require pre-trip manager approval, which should be requested with enough lead time that the manager can review before bookings are made; retroactive approval for an already-booked trip over this threshold is discouraged and may result in a request for additional justification before reimbursement.

Rental cars are reimbursable when more cost-effective than ride-hailing or taxi services for the specific trip (for example, a multi-day trip involving several client sites in a region with limited public transit), and should generally be a standard or mid-size vehicle absent a specific business reason for a larger vehicle.

## EXP-5 Meals and Entertainment

Meals while traveling on Company business are reimbursable up to $75 per day, itemized where possible; a single receipt covering multiple days should be itemized by day in the expense report notes if the daily cap is relevant to any day in the range. Client entertainment expenses require manager approval in advance and must include the business purpose and attendee list on the expense report, since client entertainment is evaluated differently from ordinary travel meals both for internal approval and for tax reporting purposes. Alcohol is reimbursable only as part of a client meal and only up to 2 drinks per attendee; alcohol purchased as part of an internal team meal without a client present is not reimbursable under this section.

## EXP-6 Submission and Approval Process

Expenses are submitted through the expense system with a receipt attached for any item over $25. Manager approval is required for all expense reports. Finance conducts a secondary review for reports over $500 or that include travel, entertainment, or equipment categories, which may add 2 to 5 business days to reimbursement timing beyond the standard processing time for smaller, single-category reports. Reimbursement is generally issued with the next regular payroll cycle following final approval.

## EXP-7 Non-Reimbursable Expenses

The following are not reimbursable under this policy: personal entertainment unrelated to Company business, traffic or parking violations, upgrades to first class or luxury accommodations, home internet or utility bills (a fixed remote-work stipend is addressed separately in a compensation communication, not this policy), and any expense already covered by the home office stipend in EXP-2 or by Company-issued equipment under EQUIPMENT. This list is illustrative rather than exhaustive; an expense not listed here is not automatically reimbursable, and the general principles in EXP-1 still apply.

## EXP-8 Recordkeeping and Audit

Finance retains expense records for 7 years for tax and audit purposes. Expense reports may be selected for random audit; employees should retain original receipts for at least 12 months after submission in case additional documentation is requested, even after an expense has already been reimbursed, since a subsequent audit can still ask for supporting documentation.

## EXP-9 Illustrative Examples

- An employee purchases an ergonomic chair for $349 as part of their initial home office setup within their first 90 days. This falls within the $500 initial stipend under EXP-2 and is approved.
- An employee purchases a second monitor for $310 under the ongoing annual stipend, which is capped at $250 per year. The claim is approved up to $250, with the remaining $60 denied absent a separate, pre-discussed exception.
- An employee submits a personal laptop purchase for reimbursement. This is denied under EXP-3 because core computing equipment is issued by IT, and the employee is directed to submit an equipment request under EQ-2 instead.
- An employee expenses a $15/month diagramming tool subscription with manager approval. Because this is under the $50/month threshold in EXP-3, no separate IT licensing review is required.
