# Workplace Conduct and Anti-Harassment Policy

## CONDUCT-1 Code of Conduct Overview

Northbridge expects all employees to act with honesty, respect, and professionalism in all work interactions, whether in a Company office, remote, or at a client site. This includes accurate reporting of work location, hours, and expenses, and honest communication with managers, HR, and clients. This general expectation of honesty is referenced throughout this policy corpus in connection with specific reporting obligations — for example, the work location reporting duty in TAX-4, the security incident reporting duty in SEC-6, and the expense documentation requirements in EXP-6 — because those specific obligations are all instances of the same underlying expectation described in this section, rather than unrelated rules.

Violations of this policy, including violations of other policies referenced here (such as the Information Security Policy or Tax and Work Location Policy), are addressed through the process in CONDUCT-4 and may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination. The severity of any resulting action depends on factors including whether the conduct was a first instance or repeated, whether it was disclosed voluntarily or discovered, and the actual or potential impact on colleagues, clients, or the Company.

## CONDUCT-2 Anti-Harassment and Anti-Discrimination

Northbridge prohibits harassment and discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, age, disability, veteran status, or any other characteristic protected by applicable law. This applies to interactions with coworkers, managers, clients, and vendors, and applies equally to in-person, virtual, and written communication (including chat and email). Harassment can include unwelcome jokes, comments, or images related to a protected characteristic, even where no single incident alone would meet a formal legal standard; the Company's standard under this policy is broader than the minimum legal threshold, so that patterns of conduct can be addressed before they escalate.

Northbridge's expectations under this section apply to Northbridge employees working at a client site or on a client engagement to the same extent as they apply in a Company office; an employee does not lose the protection of this policy, nor the obligation to comply with it, simply because they are physically working from a client's premises.

## CONDUCT-3 Reporting Procedures

Employees who experience or witness conduct that may violate this policy should report it to their manager, any People Operations team member, or through the confidential HR ticket system (see HR-OPS-2), which allows for anonymous submission where local law permits. Reports involving a direct manager should be made to that manager's manager or directly to People Operations. There is no requirement to raise a concern informally with the person involved before reporting; an employee may go directly to a formal report if that is what they are comfortable with.

Reports involving a client contact rather than a Northbridge employee follow the same internal reporting channel; People Operations and the relevant engagement lead jointly determine how to address a concern involving a client contact, which may include raising the concern with the client organization directly.

## CONDUCT-4 Investigation Process

People Operations, in coordination with Legal where appropriate, reviews all reports and determines whether a formal investigation is warranted, generally within 5 business days of a report. Investigations are conducted discreetly, with information shared only with those who need to know to conduct the investigation and reach a resolution. Both the reporting individual and the individual accused of misconduct are given an opportunity to share their perspective. Investigation outcomes are communicated to the reporting individual to the extent consistent with the privacy of all parties involved; this generally means the reporting individual is told whether the matter was substantiated and, at a high level, what action was taken, without being given the full details of any disciplinary action taken against another employee, which is treated as Confidential per SEC-3 and HR-OPS-4.

## CONDUCT-5 Retaliation Prohibition

Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith, participates in an investigation, or opposes conduct they reasonably believe violates this policy is itself a serious violation of Company policy, regardless of the outcome of the underlying investigation. Reports of retaliation are treated with the same urgency as the original report and follow the same reporting channels in CONDUCT-3. "Good faith" does not require that the underlying concern turn out to be substantiated; an employee who reports a concern they reasonably believed to be accurate is protected from retaliation even if the investigation does not substantiate the concern.

## CONDUCT-6 Conflicts of Interest

Employees must disclose any outside business activity, financial interest, or personal relationship that could reasonably create a conflict of interest with their Company responsibilities, including relationships with clients, vendors, or direct reports. Disclosures are made to People Operations through an HR ticket and are reviewed to determine whether any mitigation (such as reassignment of reporting lines) is needed. A romantic or family relationship between an employee and their direct manager, in particular, should be disclosed promptly so that the reporting relationship can be adjusted if needed, both to protect the employees involved and to maintain fairness for other team members.

## CONDUCT-7 Workplace Violence Prevention

Northbridge has zero tolerance for threats or acts of violence in the workplace, at client sites, or in any work-related setting, including remote work interactions. Any threat, regardless of how it is communicated, should be reported immediately to People Operations and, if there is an imminent safety risk, to local law enforcement. This policy is not subject to the standard 5-business-day investigation timeline in CONDUCT-4; imminent safety concerns are escalated immediately per HR-OPS-3, and People Operations coordinates with building security or local authorities as appropriate to the specific situation rather than following the standard investigation process described in CONDUCT-4.

## CONDUCT-8 Illustrative Examples

The following examples are illustrative and do not expand or narrow the rules above:

- A team member makes repeated jokes referencing a coworker's accent in team meetings. Even though no single joke rises to an extreme level, the pattern can constitute harassment under CONDUCT-2, and the affected employee can report it under CONDUCT-3 without first raising it with the individual directly.
- An employee reports a concern about their manager's conduct to that manager's manager, as CONDUCT-3 permits, and shortly afterward is excluded from meetings they previously attended regularly. This pattern should be reported as potential retaliation under CONDUCT-5, regardless of how the original conduct concern is ultimately resolved.
- An employee's sibling works at a vendor the employee's team regularly evaluates for new contracts. This relationship should be disclosed under CONDUCT-6 even though the employee has not done anything improper, so that People Operations can assess whether any additional safeguard (such as recusing the employee from vendor selection decisions involving that vendor) is appropriate.
