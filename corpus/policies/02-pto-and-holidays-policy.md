# PTO and Holidays Policy

## PTO-1 Purpose and Eligibility

Northbridge provides paid time off ("PTO") to support rest, personal needs, and short-term illness in a single combined bank, rather than separate vacation and sick banks. Combining these categories reduces administrative overhead and gives employees flexibility to use their time as their circumstances require, without needing to characterize a given day off as "sick" or "vacation." All full-time and part-time employees are eligible for PTO starting on their first day of employment, though PTO begins accruing from the hire date and is not front-loaded (see PTO-2); a new hire does not have a full year's PTO available on day one. Contractors and temporary agency staff are not eligible for PTO under this policy, since they are engaged under separate contractor agreements that address their own time-off terms, if any.

Eligibility under this policy is independent of an employee's performance rating, tenure beyond the accrual mechanics in PTO-2, or department. An employee on a performance improvement plan remains fully eligible for PTO on the same terms as any other employee in their employment classification.

## PTO-2 Accrual Rates by Employment Type

PTO accrues monthly and is credited on the last business day of each month:

- **Full-time exempt employees** accrue 1.25 days per month (15 days per year).
- **Full-time non-exempt employees** accrue 1.0 day per month (12 days per year).
- **Part-time employees** accrue a prorated amount based on their scheduled weekly hours relative to a 40-hour week (for example, an employee scheduled for 20 hours per week accrues 50% of the applicable full-time rate, and an employee scheduled for 24 hours per week accrues 60% of the applicable rate).

New hires accrue their first partial month based on the number of calendar days worked in that month, calculated as (days worked in hire month ÷ total days in hire month) × the applicable monthly rate. For example, an employee hired on the 20th of a 30-day month accrues 11/30 of their monthly rate for that first month. PTO balances, accrual history, and year-to-date usage are available to employees and to HR systems through the employee's PTO record (see mock data schema `pto_balances`), which reflects accrual through the most recently completed month; accrual for the current, in-progress month is not reflected until it posts on the last business day.

If an employee's employment type changes mid-year (for example, a part-time employee moves to a full-time role), their accrual rate changes prospectively from the effective date of the change; PTO already accrued at the prior rate is not recalculated retroactively.

## PTO-3 Requesting and Approving PTO

Employees should submit PTO requests through the HR portal at least 5 business days before the requested dates. Requests for a single day may be approved with less notice at the manager's discretion. Manager approval is required for all PTO requests regardless of length; PTO is not automatically approved by submission alone, and an employee should not treat a submitted-but-unapproved request as confirmed time off.

Managers are expected to respond to PTO requests within 2 business days. A request is not confirmed until it shows an "Approved" status; employees should not make non-refundable plans based on a pending request. When approving PTO, managers should consider team coverage but should apply consistent standards across their reports and should not deny PTO requests that meet the notice requirement without a documented staffing reason. If a manager denies or wants to modify a request, they should explain the reason in the portal so the employee can adjust dates or escalate through HR-OPS-2 if they believe the denial is inconsistent with this policy.

Consecutive PTO requests of 6 or more business days should be discussed with the manager as early as possible, ideally 3 or more weeks in advance, to allow for coverage planning, but the 5-business-day minimum notice still formally applies; a manager cannot require more than 5 business days of formal notice as a condition of approval, though earlier informal notice is encouraged for longer absences so that project staffing and client commitments can be planned around the absence.

A PTO request that would leave an employee's balance negative is not permitted; employees may request only up to their available balance at the time the time off begins, calculated as of the most recent month-end accrual under PTO-2. An employee anticipating a shortfall (for example, wanting to take time off before enough PTO has accrued) should discuss options with their manager and People Operations, which may include an approved unpaid day in limited circumstances at manager discretion.

## PTO-4 Carryover and Payout Rules

Up to 5 unused PTO days may be carried over into the following calendar year. Any balance above 5 days as of December 31 is forfeited, except where required otherwise by state law (some states restrict "use it or lose it" PTO policies; where such a state law applies, Northbridge complies with the stricter state requirement for employees working in that state). Employees are encouraged to monitor their balance throughout the fourth quarter and plan remaining PTO usage accordingly; People Operations sends a reminder notification in early November to any employee projected to exceed the carryover cap at year-end.

Upon separation from the Company, accrued and unused PTO up to the carryover cap is paid out at the employee's final base rate of pay, in accordance with applicable state law. PTO payout is processed with the employee's final paycheck and is reflected separately from severance or other separation payments, where applicable.

## PTO-5 Observed Holidays

Northbridge observes the following holidays each year, on which Company offices are closed and employees (other than those on an approved holiday-coverage schedule) are not expected to work: New Year's Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day, the day after Thanksgiving, and Christmas Day. Observed holidays do not draw down an employee's PTO balance. When an observed holiday falls on a Saturday, it is observed on the preceding Friday; when it falls on a Sunday, it is observed on the following Monday.

Employees in client-facing roles who are asked by a client to work on an observed holiday should discuss holiday-coverage compensation with their manager and People Operations in advance; such arrangements are handled case by case and are not automatically included in this policy.

## PTO-6 Floating Holidays

In addition to the fixed holidays in PTO-5, full-time employees receive 2 floating holidays per calendar year to use for holidays, cultural or religious observances, or personal reasons not otherwise covered. Floating holidays are prorated for part-time employees (for example, a part-time employee at 60% of full-time schedule receives 1.2 floating holidays, rounded to the nearest half day), do not carry over year to year, and are requested the same way as PTO under PTO-3. Floating holidays are intended in part to give employees whose religious or cultural observances are not among the fixed holidays in PTO-5 equivalent flexibility, and managers should be mindful of this purpose when considering floating holiday requests around specific dates.

## PTO-7 Unplanned Absences and Emergencies

For short-term illness or family emergencies where advance notice under PTO-3 is not possible, employees should notify their manager as soon as reasonably practicable, ideally before the start of the workday, and submit the PTO request in the portal within 2 business days after returning. A brief notification by message or email is sufficient; employees are not required to describe the medical or personal details of the emergency to their manager. Repeated unplanned absences without timely notification may be addressed through the standard performance management process, separate from this policy; this policy governs the PTO balance impact of the absence, not any performance consequence of a notification failure.

## PTO-8 Interaction with Leave of Absence

PTO is intended for short-term, planned, or unplanned time off. Absences expected to last 5 or more consecutive business days for a medical, family, or parental reason should generally be handled under the Leave of Absence Policy (see LEAVE-1) rather than through sequential PTO requests, both because the Leave of Absence Policy provides job protections that this policy does not, and because it allows People Operations to plan for a longer absence appropriately. Employees may use available PTO to cover unpaid portions of an approved leave of absence, as described in LEAVE-7; using PTO in this way draws down the same single PTO bank described in PTO-2, so an employee who exhausts their PTO balance during a leave transitions to unpaid leave status for any remaining unpaid portion of that leave.

## PTO-9 Blackout Periods

Certain periods each year carry heavier client and internal deadlines, and PTO requests during these windows are subject to additional review rather than the standard manager-discretion process in PTO-3. Northbridge designates the following as standing blackout periods, reviewed and, if needed, adjusted by People Operations each November for the following calendar year:

- **Fiscal year-end close**: the last 5 business days of December and the first 3 business days of January, company-wide, for all departments.
- **Department- or client-specific blackout windows**: Consulting Delivery may designate additional blackout dates tied to a specific client's go-live or reporting deadline; these are recorded against the affected employees' records and communicated by the Director of Consulting Delivery at least 30 days in advance.

A PTO request that falls, in whole or in part, within an applicable blackout period is not automatically denied, but requires approval from the employee's manager **and** a Department Head (rather than manager approval alone), and should be submitted at least 10 business days in advance rather than the standard 5 under PTO-3. Department Head approval considers overall team coverage during the blackout window, not just the individual request. An employee whose blackout-period request is denied should be offered the option to take the time immediately before or after the blackout window instead, where feasible.

Blackout periods do not reduce or forfeit any PTO balance; they only add an additional approval step and a longer notice requirement for requests that fall within them.

## PTO-10 Frequently Encountered Situations

The following situations illustrate how the sections above apply together, though they do not modify the policy itself:

- **A PTO request spanning a Company holiday.** If an employee requests PTO for a stretch of days that includes an observed holiday under PTO-5, the holiday itself is not deducted from the PTO balance; only the non-holiday business days within the requested range are deducted.
- **A request submitted with 3 business days' notice for a single day off.** This is within the manager's discretion under PTO-3 and does not require any special exception process; the manager may approve, and commonly does for single-day requests.
- **An employee with a zero or near-zero balance requesting time off before their next accrual posts.** Per PTO-3, the request can only be approved up to the currently accrued balance; the employee should either wait for the next month-end accrual, request a shorter period, or discuss an unpaid day with their manager and People Operations.
- **A part-time employee whose scheduled hours change mid-year.** Their accrual rate under PTO-2 updates prospectively from the effective date of the schedule change, and their existing balance is not recalculated.
