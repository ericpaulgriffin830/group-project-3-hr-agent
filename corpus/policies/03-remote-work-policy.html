Remote Work Policy

Document ID: REMOTE-WORK · Version 4.1 · Effective 2026-01-05 · Owner: People Operations / Legal · Applies to: All employees

RW-1 Work Arrangement Categories

Every employee is classified into one of three work arrangements, recorded in the HR system: onsite (primarily working from a Company office, generally 4 or more days per week), hybrid (splitting time between a Company office and another location, typically the employee's home, on a schedule agreed with their manager), or remote (working full-time from a location other than a Company office, with no expectation of regular office attendance). Classification is set at hire per ONB-5 and can be changed by mutual agreement between employee and manager, recorded through an HR ticket. A work arrangement classification is distinct from, and does not by itself authorize, a specific physical work location; the location rules in this policy apply regardless of an employee's onsite, hybrid, or remote classification.

Work arrangement classification affects several other policies referenced throughout this corpus: it determines home office stipend eligibility under EXP-2, it is one factor IT considers when provisioning equipment under EQ-1, and it is recorded alongside employment type in the mock employee data used by HR tools and by this policy corpus's example scenarios.

RW-2 Approved Remote Work States

Hybrid and remote employees may work from any state where Northbridge is registered to run payroll, as listed in TAX-2 (Illinois, Indiana, Wisconsin, Texas, California, New York, Washington, Colorado, Georgia, Florida, North Carolina, and Arizona), without additional approval beyond their standard work arrangement classification. This list is maintained by Finance and Legal and can change; the authoritative, current list always lives in TAX-2 rather than being duplicated and potentially going stale in this document. Working from any other state, even temporarily, is governed by RW-3.

An employee whose standing work arrangement is "remote" is expected to designate a single primary work location within a Registered State, recorded as their home state in the HR system, which is used for ongoing payroll tax withholding. This is distinct from the temporary travel scenario addressed in RW-3, which covers short stays away from that primary location.

RW-3 Temporary Remote Work From a Non-Home Location (Domestic)

Employees may work from a non-home domestic location (for example, while visiting family or traveling) for up to 10 consecutive business days without prior approval, provided the location is within a Registered State listed in TAX-2. This 10-day allowance may be used more than once per year, but should not be used as a way to establish what is functionally a new ongoing work location without going through the process below; if a "temporary" arrangement is expected to recur regularly (for example, every month), it should instead be discussed with the manager as a potential change to the employee's standing work arrangement.

Working from a domestic location for more than 10 consecutive business days, or from any state not listed in TAX-2 for any length of time, requires a Remote Work Location Change request submitted at least 3 weeks in advance through the HR portal. The request should include the destination location, the requested date range, and the business or personal reason for the request (the reason does not need to meet any particular bar of necessity; this policy does not restrict remote work location changes to hardship situations, but the reason helps Finance and Legal assess urgency). Finance and Legal review the request under TAX-3 to determine whether the Company can register in the new state within the requested timeframe. Requests are approved, denied, or approved with a modified timeline; the requesting employee is notified in writing of the outcome, generally within the 5-business-day target described in HR-OPS-2 for this ticket category, though complex registration questions can take longer given the 2-to-6-week registration timeline referenced in TAX-3.

If a domestic Remote Work Location Change request is approved, the approval is time-bound to the requested (or modified) date range. An employee who wishes to extend beyond the approved end date must submit a new request rather than assuming the original approval continues indefinitely.

RW-4 International Remote Work

Working from outside the United States requires an approved International Remote Work Exception, regardless of duration; there is no equivalent to the 10-business-day unapproved allowance in RW-3 for international travel, because even a short period of international remote work can raise immigration, tax treaty, and data residency questions that a domestic short stay does not. Exceptions are limited to a maximum of 20 business days per calendar year per employee, per TAX-5, and require sign-off from People Operations, Legal, and IT Security (for the data residency review in SEC-3). The 20-business-day cap applies to the employee's total international remote work for the calendar year, whether taken in one stretch or across multiple shorter trips; an employee who has already used 12 international remote work days earlier in the year has only 8 remaining days available under this exception process for the rest of that year.

Requests must be submitted at least 4 weeks in advance, longer than the 3-week minimum for domestic requests, because international review typically involves additional considerations: whether the destination country has a tax treaty with the United States relevant to short-term business travelers, whether the employee's role and the destination country's laws create a risk that the Company could be deemed to have a taxable business presence ("permanent establishment") in that country, and whether the destination country has data protection or data localization requirements that affect how the employee may access Company or client systems while there.

Requests for international remote work exceeding 20 business days in a single stretch, or that would exceed 20 business days for the employee across the calendar year when combined with prior international remote work in that year, are not approved under the standard exception process described in this section; the employee's manager and People Operations should discuss alternatives such as a shorter trip, use of PTO for the portion of the trip beyond what could be worked, or, in unusual circumstances, a formal individual agreement negotiated directly by Legal that addresses the additional tax and immigration considerations a longer or recurring international arrangement would raise. Such individual agreements are not part of the standard exception process and are granted rarely, generally only where the Company has an independent business reason (such as an international client engagement requiring on-the-ground presence) rather than solely at employee request.

RW-5 Security Requirements for Remote Work

All remote and hybrid work must comply with the Information Security Policy (INFOSEC), including VPN use for Confidential data (SEC-4) and multi-factor authentication (SEC-5). Employees approved for temporary remote work under RW-3 or an International Remote Work Exception under RW-4 should confirm with IT Security whether a loaner device is advisable for the trip, particularly for international travel, given the border inspection considerations noted in SEC-4. IT Security's response to a loaner device request is not a condition of Remote Work Location Change approval under RW-3 or RW-4, but employees are strongly encouraged to raise it early enough that a loaner device can be prepared and shipped before departure if recommended.

RW-6 Approval Process and Roles

The employee's manager is the first approver for any Remote Work Location Change request or International Remote Work Exception, confirming business feasibility (for example, that client commitments and team coverage can accommodate the requested dates). Finance and Legal then review for tax and registration risk (domestic, per TAX-3) or tax treaty, immigration, and permanent-establishment risk (international, per TAX-5). IT Security reviews for data residency and device security considerations described in SEC-3 and SEC-4. All three approvals are required before a change is considered approved; a manager's approval alone is not sufficient authorization to work from a new location under this policy, and an employee should not begin working from the new location based on manager approval alone while the Finance/Legal and IT Security reviews are still pending.

Where any one of the three approvers denies a request, the request as a whole is denied, though the employee may resubmit a modified request (for example, shorter dates, or a different destination) that addresses the specific concern raised.

RW-7 Equipment and Expense Considerations

Temporary or exception-based remote work does not change equipment issuance under EQUIPMENT or expense eligibility under EXPENSE. Employees should not expect an additional home office stipend under EXP-2 for a temporary location change; that stipend applies once, tied to an employee's standing hybrid or remote classification under RW-1, not to each temporary location an employee works from. Reasonable incidental costs directly caused by an approved international remote work arrangement (such as a local SIM card needed to maintain secure connectivity) may be discussed with the manager and submitted under the general expense principles in EXP-1, though this policy does not create a new stipend category for this purpose.

RW-8 Non-Compliance Consequences

Working from an unapproved location, or continuing to work from a temporary location beyond an approved window, may result in suspension of remote work privileges pending review, a requirement to return to a Registered State or Company office at the employee's expense, and, in cases of deliberate non-disclosure, review under the Workplace Conduct policy (CONDUCT-1). Consistent with TAX-6, the employee may also bear personal responsibility for resulting state tax consequences. A single inadvertent lapse (for example, working an extra day or two past an approved window due to a delayed return flight) is generally addressed informally between the employee, manager, and People Operations rather than treated as a policy violation, provided the employee promptly discloses the situation; the concern this section addresses is undisclosed or repeated non-compliance, not good-faith minor deviations that are proactively reported.

RW-9 Frequently Asked Questions

Can I work from a coffee shop or co-working space instead of my home? Yes, as long as it is within the same state as your registered work location and you follow the security requirements in RW-5, including VPN use for Confidential data over shared or public Wi-Fi. Working from a co-working space in a different state follows the same rules as any other domestic location change under RW-3.

Does a weekend trip count toward the 10-business-day domestic allowance? No. The 10-business-day allowance in RW-3 is measured in business days, so a trip that spans a weekend only counts the weekdays within it, unless the employee is also working on the weekend days, which is uncommon and would need to be discussed with the manager separately.

What happens if my Remote Work Location Change request is still pending when my planned travel date arrives? The employee should not begin working from the new location until all three approvals described in RW-6 are complete. If a decision is genuinely time-sensitive, the employee should flag the urgency to their manager and People Operations as early as possible so the review can be prioritized; submitting the request at least 3 weeks in advance as required by RW-3 is the best way to avoid this situation.

Can I split a 6-week international trip into two 3-week trips to stay under the cap? No. The 20-business-day cap in TAX-5 applies to total international remote work days across the calendar year, not per trip, so splitting a trip does not change whether the aggregate exceeds the cap.
